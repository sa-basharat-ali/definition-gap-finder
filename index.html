Tonight's menu

served over a customer's first four weeks

Days 1–3Connect

Count before you trustReconciled sources

Connect the warehouse and SaaS tools, then reconcile row counts and key totals against each source system before modeling anything.

Done before: through my own consulting practice I found an 80% upstream data loss at a VC-backed computer vision startup that every dashboard showed as healthy, then built source reconciliation that caught two more real sync failures on its first run.

Days 3–5Discover

Find the questions nobody has definedDefinition gap report

List what leadership will ask, compute each question under every defensible definition, and rank by how far apart the answers are. The report below is this step.

Done before: in a semantic-layer audit I built, 40 of 90 metric questions (44%) had no single right answer until a rule was declared.

Week 2Define

One definition per question, owned by the customerApproved semantic model

Walk the customer's data owner through the top of the report. Every choice is theirs, written once into the semantic model, with what it includes and excludes.

Done before: at EY I designed the internal reporting layer partners and senior directors used; at Geidea I built a reconciliation reporting platform with shared definitions across 5+ teams.

Weeks 2–3Validate

Golden answers and nightly testsSigned-off answers

For each settled question, the owner signs off one golden answer. A test reruns it every night, so a schema change or a broken sync shows up as a failed test, not a wrong number in a meeting.

Done before: an 11-model dbt migration on a multi-tenant Snowflake warehouse (50+ client schemas), with observability built from zero.

WeeklyOngoing

Drift, new questions, product feedbackFewer repeat issues

Watch for drift and stalled syncs, triage new questions into the model, and turn recurring customer issues into concrete feedback for product and engineering.

Done before: on Geidea's executive strategy team, the churn and revenue models behind a tiered retention strategy cut merchant churn from 35% to about 10%.

the second course, served

A definition gap report

Synthetic B2B SaaS customer: 600 accounts, Stripe subscriptions and 2,607 invoices with discounts, trials, annual prepay, refunds and euros, 466 Salesforce opportunities, monthly usage. Click a question.

The part I got wrong

My first synthetic customer made churn a one-time coin flip, so this month's churn read 0.0% under every definition, and the report would have marked churn as settled. Agreement between definitions can mean the data is broken, not that the metric is clear. That's why course one comes before course two.
